The Friedman vs. Stakeholder Dilemma

Foundational Corporate Governance & Institutional Fiduciary Duties

In his seminal 1970 essay for The New York Times Magazine, Milton Friedman articulated what would become the preeminent shareholder-first doctrine: the social responsibility of business encompasses exclusively the maximization of capital returns within legal parameters.

“There is one and only one social responsibility of business—to use its resources and engage in activities designed to increase its profits so long as it stays within the rules of the game.”

Conversely, R. Edward Freeman (1984) proposed the stakeholder model, arguing that sustainable long-term value cannot be extracted in a vacuum. Under Freeman’s paradigm, the firm is an interdependent nexus answering simultaneously to employees, suppliers, environmental ecosystems, and the civic communities hosting industrial facilities.
